Rate Limiter

AI demand is lifting utility spending, but regulators decide who earns on it

Liam Kranenburg, 25 November 2026. No. 5, decision brief, US power and utilities. Version 1.1.

Key implications

Disclosure

It names companies to explain regulation, not to recommend securities. Nothing in it is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security. Calls concern regulatory and price records, never share prices. To the author’s knowledge, the author holds no individual shares in the companies named (index funds aside).

Between 28 October and 6 November, four of the five utility groups serving the busiest US data center markets raised their capital plans or sales outlooks; the largest plan, AEP's, rose to $100 billion for 2027–31. In times past, a utility's earnings grew with its rate base, the assets on which regulators let it earn a set return. Yet regulators also weigh the household bill. Virginia granted Dominion a 9.8% return in November 2025, not the 10.4% it sought. Georgia froze base rates through 2028, and on 3 November its voters gave Democrats control of the commission that sets them. KPA analysis of EIA data shows the pressure on that bill. From 2019 to 2025, residential prices in 95 vertically integrated utility territories rose 30.5%, against 25.9% inflation, and about as much where sales grew fast as where they fell. On company guidance, sales keep pace with rate base at some of these utilities and fall well short at others.

  • Data centers have enlarged utility spending plans. AEP expects its rate base to grow from $87 billion in 2026 to $162 billion in 2031, and its earnings per share by 9–11% a year. Southern has signed 21 gigawatts (GW) of large-load contracts. Gabelli Funds put electric utilities at 18 times forward earnings in June 2026, against a historical median of 16.8.
  • Spending earns only what regulators allow, and household prices have risen. On company guidance, growth in earnings per share equals 50% (Entergy) to 83% (Southern) of rate base growth. Residential prices rose about 30% whether a utility's sales rose more than 10% or fell; at Georgia Power they rose 35%.
  • Sales growth sets how much capital each megawatt-hour carries. On guidance, the capital each megawatt-hour must carry rises 38% at Southern and 37% at Entergy over five years, and falls 4% at Pinnacle West, whose sales outlook now exceeds its rate base guidance. Dominion Energy Virginia and APS held 2019–25 residential price rises close to inflation; Dominion's rose 19% in January–September 2026 (preliminary).

—November 2026

What is new in this brief

KPA searched for prior publication of each finding before release. These results were not found in that search; everything else is credited to its source.

  • Across 95 vertically integrated investor-owned utility territories, bundled residential prices rose 30.5% from 2019 to 2025, against 25.9% for consumer prices. Utilities whose sales grew more than 10% raised them 29.8%, about as much as those whose sales fell (29.2%).
  • On company guidance, growth in earnings per share equals 50% of expected rate base growth at Entergy, 60% at Dominion, 75% at Pinnacle West, 76% at AEP and 83% at Southern.
  • On company guidance, rate base per megawatt-hour sold rises 38% at Southern and 37% at Entergy over five years, while Southern's Georgia and Alabama base rates stay flat until at least 2029. At Pinnacle West it falls 4%, on rate base guidance to 2028 and sales guidance to 2030.
  • Dominion Energy Virginia and APS, whose commercial sales rose 43% and 39% from 2019 to 2025, raised residential prices 26% and 25%; Georgia Power, with commercial sales up 7%, raised them 35%.

30.5%

From 2019 to 2025, bundled residential prices in 95 vertically integrated utility territories rose 30.5%, against 25.9% for consumer prices, and about as much where sales grew fast as where they fell.

Utilities whose sales grew more than 10% raised residential prices 29.8%; those whose sales fell raised them 29.2%. The fitted slope is −0.12 (p = 0.34). The comparison is descriptive; fuel, storms, freezes and the timing of rate cases also move prices.

KPA's own calculation from EIA Form 861 (final 2019 and 2025 data) and BLS CPI-U, at the level of individual utility territories. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and The Brattle Group (Retail Price Trends, 2026 edition, July update) published the state-level comparison of load growth and retail prices earlier, finding lower real prices across all customers in faster-growing states with a weaker link for households, and are credited.
