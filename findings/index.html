No. 2, 29 October 2026

Slot Machine

GE Vernova has 122 GW of gas turbines on firm order or reserved, yet planned US gas capacity has risen 3.5 times in two years while the share under construction fell from 26% to 18%. KPA analysis of EIA's own lists shows why the gap matters, and who pays for the slots that outlive their plants.

41%

EIA's status codes lag the field: 41% of the gas it lists as not yet seeking approval is already approved or under construction.

KPA checked each of the 35 plants behind the 37.4 GW that EIA lists for 2027–30 as not yet seeking approval, against regulators' orders, utility filings and company statements. Of that capacity, 15.4 GW, or 41%, is already approved or under construction. Another 5.8 GW is before regulators.

KPA's own analysis: a plant-by-plant check of EIA Form 860M (September 2026) against state commission dockets, utility and company releases, SEC filings and trade press.

No. 1, 26 October 2026

Load Bearing

Data centers drove roughly all US demand growth for a decade. The plants to serve them arrive after the forecasts that justify them.

~100%

Data centers were roughly all net US demand growth, 2014–23.

Between 2014 and 2023, US data centers increased their electricity use by about 118 TWh. Over the same nine years, total US retail sales rose by only about 110 TWh. Everything else combined was essentially flat, within 1%. Ending the comparison in 2025, data centers account for about 60% of growth.

KPA calculation on Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and EIA data. Range across estimates: 73–108%. The general point, that data centers drive growth, was published by EIA and IEA.
