No. 5, 25 November 2026

Rate Limiter

Between 28 October and 6 November, four of the five utility groups serving the busiest US data center markets raised their capital plans or sales outlooks; the largest plan, AEP's, rose to $100 billion for 2027–31. From 2019 to 2025, residential prices in 95 vertically integrated utility territories rose 30.5%, against 25.9% inflation, and about as much where sales grew fast as where they fell.

30.5%

From 2019 to 2025, bundled residential prices in 95 vertically integrated utility territories rose 30.5%, against 25.9% for consumer prices, and about as much where sales grew fast as where they fell.

Utilities whose sales grew more than 10% raised residential prices 29.8%; those whose sales fell raised them 29.2%. The fitted slope is −0.12 (p = 0.34). The comparison is descriptive; fuel, storms, freezes and the timing of rate cases also move prices.

KPA's own calculation from EIA Form 861 (final 2019 and 2025 data) and BLS CPI-U, at the level of individual utility territories. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and The Brattle Group (Retail Price Trends, 2026 edition, July update) published the state-level comparison of load growth and retail prices earlier, finding lower real prices across all customers in faster-growing states with a weaker link for households, and are credited.

No. 4, 25 November 2026

Cold Storage

On 18 November 2026, NERC reported that new batteries had added more to US winter capacity than any other resource: 9.2 GW at the peak hour, from 20.6 GW of nameplate; during Winter Storm Fern, Texas net load stayed within 10% of its peak for 16 straight hours, and at summer 2025's peak it did so for four. KPA analysis of EIA data shows the effect: at the peaks of winter 2025–26, the October 2026 fleets in California and the Southwest could have held peak net load down by at most 43% and 47% of their rating, against 72% and 76% in summer, while NERC counts both at 85%.

43%

At the peaks of winter 2025–26, California's battery fleet could have cut peak net load by at most 43% of its rating, against 72% at summer 2025's peak.

KPA tested how far each of the six largest battery fleets, as they stood in October 2026, could have pushed down each season's peak net load. California's could have cut the winter peak by 43% of its rating and the summer one by 72%; the four large Southwest utilities' fleets, by 47% and 76%. NERC counts batteries in both regions at 85%.

KPA's own calculation from EIA-930 hourly data and EIA Form 860M (October 2026), compared with NERC's assessment-area counts. The method follows Denholm and others (NREL, 2020) and Mills and Rodriguez (LBNL, 2020), and the modelled ELCC studies by Astrapé for ERCOT and E3 for the Desert Southwest are credited; the regional winter and summer limits were not found in KPA's prior-publication search.

No. 3, 10 November 2026

Banked Fires

In 2016–24, 88% of the coal capacity EIA listed a year ahead retired in the year listed; in 2025, 27% did, and none of the 6.4 GW listed for 2026 had retired by the end of September. KPA analysis shows who is holding the plants, and which ones run: units held by federal orders ran at 22% of capacity in January–August 2026, and units their owners kept ran at 38%.

22%

Coal held open by federal order ran at 22% of capacity in January–August 2026; coal its owners kept ran at 38%.

KPA matched every delayed unit to DOE's orders and its owner's filings, then measured its output in EIA's generator data through August. The units held by orders ran at 22% of capacity in January–August 2026, down from 49% in the same months of 2022–24. Units their owners kept ran at 38%, up from 34%, against 47% for the US coal fleet.

KPA's comparison of ordered units, owner-kept units and the fleet, from EIA-923 and EIA Form 860M data (fleet figure from EIA's Electric Power Monthly). Falling output at the ordered plants was reported earlier by Utility Dive (23 June 2026), Argus (30 March 2026) and MV2 (19 October 2026), and is credited.

No. 2, 29 October 2026

Slot Machine

GE Vernova has 122 GW of gas turbines on firm order or reserved, yet planned US gas capacity has risen 3.5 times in two years while the share under construction fell from 26% to 18%. KPA analysis of EIA's own lists shows why the gap matters, and who pays for the slots that outlive their plants.

41%

EIA's status codes lag the field: 41% of the gas it lists as not yet seeking approval is already approved or under construction.

KPA checked each of the 35 plants behind the 37.4 GW that EIA lists for 2027–30 as not yet seeking approval, against regulators' orders, utility filings and company statements. Of that capacity, 15.4 GW, or 41%, is already approved or under construction. Another 5.8 GW is before regulators.

KPA's own analysis: a plant-by-plant check of EIA Form 860M (September 2026) against state commission dockets, utility and company releases, SEC filings and trade press.

No. 1, 26 October 2026

Load Bearing

Data centers drove roughly all US demand growth for a decade. The plants to serve them arrive after the forecasts that justify them.

~100%

Data centers were roughly all net US demand growth, 2014–23.

Between 2014 and 2023, US data centers increased their electricity use by about 118 TWh. Over the same nine years, total US retail sales rose by only about 110 TWh. Everything else combined was essentially flat, within 1%. Ending the comparison in 2025, data centers account for about 60% of growth.

KPA calculation on Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and EIA data. Range across estimates: 73–108%. The general point, that data centers drive growth, was published by EIA and IEA.
